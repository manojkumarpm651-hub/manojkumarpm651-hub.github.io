About me

Hi, I'm Manoj. I'm an aspiring AI Product Manager from Whitefield, Bengaluru.

I've always been more curious about why a product exists than about how its code is written. Product management drew me in because it sits where that curiosity is useful: understanding how people actually use a product, what it does for them, and how to shape it with the clients who depend on it.

I studied computer applications, with a BCA and an MCA. I'm not a full-time coder, but I'm solid on the fundamentals. I can read how a system fits together, talk through trade-offs with engineers, and tell whether an idea is feasible before a team commits to it. Where I'm strongest is understanding the product itself: who it's for, the problem it solves, and how to manage it over time.

I completed the Future-Ready Product Management with Applied AI programme from Masai School and IIT Roorkee, where my capstone scored 9.4 out of 10. Since then I've been building a portfolio that shows how I think: one open decision per case, primary sources behind every number, and a working prototype where the decision depends on AI.

Right nowWriting a hard-tradeoff memo on Tesla's Supercharger network, and building an AI tool that scans websites for phishing threats.

Snap-to-Stock 2.0

B2B SaaS · Indian MSMEs · Vyapar
The decision

Vyapar already ships a bill scanner. What would it take to turn a photo of a supplier bill into trusted inventory without the shop owner re-typing it?

GST compliance costs Indian MSMEs 3.6–4.2% of turnover against a 1–2% global benchmark, and the April 2026 ITC hard-block raised the cost of a wrong entry. The redesign covers Vyapar's vendor purchase recording flow: photo, structured line items, split-screen review, commit to inventory.

How I work

Define the fork first

Every case starts with one decision that is still open. If management has already answered it in public, I retire it instead of arguing past it.

Primary sources carry the weight

Shareholder letters, filings and earnings calls hold up the argument. SEO-grade sources never do.

Run the numbers

Every derivation is reproduced in code and checked against stated figures. The scripts fail loudly if a check breaks.

Put corrections on the record

Errors I find, mine or the public record's, are documented in the work. Known limits are disclosed, not polished away.

Mark built versus designed

A prototype and a roadmap are different claims. Each build says which parts run today and which are plans.

Ship safely in public

Keys rotated and secrets moved to environment variables before any repo goes public. Row-level security checked on every table.
